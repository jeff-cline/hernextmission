VETERAN IDENTITY DISORDER

Not a clinical diagnosis. A real, recognized phenomenon — the loss of self that arrives the day after the uniform comes off.

What it is.

Veteran Identity Disorder is not in the DSM. It's not a formal diagnosis. But it is a phenomenon written about across military medicine, peer literature, and Veteran-services research: the deep loss of identity, structure, brotherhood/sisterhood, and purpose that lands when service ends. For women — already underrepresented in service culture — the cliff is steeper.

What it can look like.

  • Loss of meaning. The work mattered. What now matters?
  • Sleep, fitness, and health discipline collapsing without imposed structure.
  • Civilian life feeling fake — "these people don't know."
  • Marriage and parenting strain as the household tries to resume.
  • Depression, anxiety, isolation. Sometimes alcohol use. Sometimes worse.
  • A specific grief for the woman in uniform — like she died and someone else is wearing her clothes.

What helps.

  • Peer community. Women who get it without explanation.
  • Identity work. Coaching that integrates who you were with who you're becoming — not asking you to choose.
  • Somatic and nervous-system practice. The body has carried what the mind can't reach.
  • Clinical support when needed. A therapist who understands service — VA Vet Centers, Cohen Veterans Network, or a vetted private practice.
  • Mission again. Not the same one. A next one.

Where HER NEXT MISSION fits.

This is the work we exist for. Coaching, retreats, podcast, community — every program is built around the question of identity after the uniform. We are not clinicians. When clinical care is needed we refer to providers who specialize in women Veterans. For everything in between — the everyday rebuild — we walk it with you.

Crisis line. If you are in crisis, dial 988 and press 1 for the Veterans Crisis Line, or text 838255. 24/7, free, confidential.
